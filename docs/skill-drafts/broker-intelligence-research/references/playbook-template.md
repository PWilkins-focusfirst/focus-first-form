# The Supply Chain Accountability Playbook — Template

This is the fixed structure of the BIR flagship deliverable. Section order, verdict-box format, grading vocabulary, and table schemas do not vary between engagements — consistency across cases is part of what the client is buying. Content depth flexes with the case; the skeleton never does.

**File name:** `Supply-Chain-Accountability-Playbook-[case-slug]-[YYYY-MM-DD].docx`
**Format:** FocusFirst professional — navy `#1F3864` section headers, gold `#C8A951` accent rules, `CONFIDENTIAL — ATTORNEY WORK PRODUCT` header on every page, FocusFirst footer with page numbers, Calibri 11pt body, dates in YYYY-MM-DD.
**Length guidance:** 15–30 pages. Under 15 usually means a module was under-run; over 30 means module detail is being duplicated instead of cited. Module artifacts (fitness snapshot, broker memo, screen report, OSI report) travel alongside the Playbook — the Playbook synthesizes and cites, it does not reproduce.
**Voice:** practitioner voice throughout — plain trial-lawyer language, no framework jargon, no methodology talk in the body (methodology lives in §10). Write for a busy trial lawyer: the answer in the first five lines of every section, the proof beneath it.

## § 1 — Accountability Verdict (one page, always page 1 after cover)

Four fixed elements, in order:

**1a. The Scheme** — three sentences, no more. What happened above the driver: who tendered, who looked away, how the architecture guaranteed nobody would check. This is the paragraph the client lawyer reads aloud to their partner.

**1b. The Blind-Eye Table** — one row per party in the chain (skip none, including parties graded clean):

| Party (role) | What they could have seen | What they did instead | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|

"What they could have seen" is date-of-loss specific and sourced (e.g., "3 involuntary revocations on the L&I record; 41% vehicle OOS rate vs. ~22% national — visible on the load date"). "What they did instead" is the blind-eye act (tendered anyway; never pulled the record; ran the checkbox vet; designed the layer so it had nothing to check). Grade column uses the standard vocabulary only.

**1c. The Leverage Line** — one sentence naming the insurance/exposure architecture this record reaches (e.g., carrier minimum vs. broker contingent tower vs. network self-insured layer), sourced to the module findings. State only what the record supports.

**1d. Priority Actions** — exactly three, imperative, each with its deadline or trigger. These repeat as the first three items of § 7.

**Grading vocabulary (used everywhere in the document):**
- **CONFIRMED** — the fact appears in a document, sworn testimony, or a government record of record.
- **INDICATED** — at least one positive documentary tell, no innocent explanation produced.
- **NOT INDICATED** — the record affirmatively reconciles; say so plainly (clean findings buy credibility for the adverse ones).
- **UNRESOLVED** — the dispositive record has not been produced; the Truth Ledger row and Pressure Campaign ask that resolve it are cross-referenced by number.

Never grade above the evidence. A Playbook that overstates once is a Playbook the defense reads for comfort.

## § 3 — The Blind-Eye Findings

One subsection per scheme found, drawn from this fixed taxonomy (name the scheme in the header; a case typically presents one to three):

1. **Negligent selection** — a selecting party (broker, shipper, network) tendered to a carrier whose public record showed unfitness on the load date.
2. **Double-brokering / unlawful re-tender** — the carrier-of-record arranged rather than hauled (double-broker-screen verdict imported verbatim).
3. **Chameleon / reincarnation** — the operating entity is a prior bad actor re-skinned (shared address/officers/factor; serial dockets).
4. **Network insulation** — a network giant exercising operational control (routing, metrics, scanners, termination) through an entity architecture built to disclaim responsibility.
5. **Lease / misclassification theater** — Part 376 lease absent or cosmetic; driver-carrier-equipment relationships that exist on paper only, or paper that exists to hide the real relationship.
6. **Safety-data suppression** — a party that collected safety data (telematics, scorecards, vetting-vendor alerts, prior complaints) and buried or overrode it.
7. **Undisclosed affiliation** — entities sharing address, phone, email, officers, equipment or financing that were presented to the selector, the insurer, or FMCSA as unrelated. *The finding is the non-disclosure, not the overlap — operating multiple entities and holding both carrier and broker authority are legal.*
8. **Asset flight / successor evasion** — equipment, authority or business continuing under a new entity after the loss, with the predecessor left as the judgment-proof defendant (module 11).
9. **Driver-qualification failure** — the **carrier** put a driver behind the wheel it had no lawful basis to qualify: credential defects, a void medical certificate, missing MVRs, PSP or Clearinghouse queries (module 12). *Attaches to the carrier. It attaches to a selector only for the parts that were **publicly visible** — an ELP out-of-service event or a Part 391 pattern in SMS.*

For each scheme present: the finding and grade in the first line; the evidence (each item cited to a module artifact, Bates number, or saved raw pull); the party it attaches to; and the one document that would upgrade the grade. Schemes screened as NOT INDICATED get one line each in a closing paragraph — the clean screens are stated, not hidden.

**Grade UNRESOLVED, not NOT INDICATED, where the analysis was not run.** An unrun equipment graph or an unverified examiner registry is a gap, not a clean screen, and the distinction is the first thing a defense lawyer will test in §10.

## § 4 — The Carrier Fitness Record

*Where module 12 ran, the Part 391 violation pattern and any ELP or medical out-of-service events belong here as part of the carrier's roadside record — stated against the carrier, per `driver-qualification.md` §0.*

The date-of-loss breach story from the carrier-fitness-record module, summarized to two pages maximum: the two-column (date-of-loss vs. current) headline rows; the itemized-record highlights (worst inspections, OOS rate vs. ~22% national average, violation categories in plain English, crash history with the reporting-lag note); revocation/insurance-lapse history; chameleon findings. Cite the full Carrier Fitness Snapshot by filename. Frame every figure as *what a diligent selecting party would have seen that day*. Repeat per carrier-entity if the chain has more than one.

## § 5 — Pattern, Monitors & Ongoing Danger

- OSI pass results scoped to the chain entities: prior incidents (PRE / PERI / POST the client's crash), regulatory history, safety-culture intelligence, prior litigation including any prior broker co-defendants (the Boehne-blueprint check).
- Standing-monitor intelligence on the entities (from the monitor feed): reputation flags, double-broker alerts, insider commentary — **always labeled as unverified intelligence** with its authentication route stated.
- The ongoing-danger paragraph: is the scheme still running today, and what shows it. This is the seed the focus group will grow.

## § 6 — The Truth Ledger (the centerpiece)

One table, numbered rows, ordered by leverage. Schema is fixed:

| # | Record | Holder | Why it exists | What it will show | Vehicle | Trigger/Timing |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

- **Record** — the specific document/data category (rate confirmation; carrier vetting file; Relay scorecard; load/tender history 24 months; factor remittance ledger; TMS tender log; ELD file; Part 376 leases; monitoring-vendor alert log with overrides).
- **Holder** — the entity (and where known, the custodian role) that possesses it.
- **Why it exists** — the business reality that guarantees the record exists (they cannot pay a carrier without a rate con; the TMS logs every tender; the factor keeps a remittance ledger because that is its business). This column is what makes denial untenable.
- **What it will show** — stated as expectation, graded language, tied to the § 3 finding it will confirm or resolve.
- **Vehicle** — the procedural instrument: RFP to party / Rule 45 to named non-party / 30(b)(6) topic / FOIA / certified agency record.
- **Trigger/Timing** — when it issues (day 0, with complaint, on deficiency, before depo X).

Every UNRESOLVED grade in §§ 1–5 must have a Truth Ledger row. Every row must reappear in § 7 as an executed ask.

## § 7 — The Pressure Campaign

The sequenced procedural roadmap, in windows. For each item: the instrument, the target, the Truth Ledger rows it collects (by number), and ready-to-adapt language or a pointer to the module's template (broker-intelligence `discovery-templates.md`; double-broker-screen Phase 5 set; network-giants per-network target list).

- **Day 0 (now / pre-filing):** preservation letters to every chain entity and the network program office; UCC-1 pulls; certified-record orders (MCMIS, month-of-loss SMS snapshot); crash-report and public-record gap-fills.
- **Days 1–30 (filing wave):** complaint posture recommendation (who is named now vs. after discovery); first-wave carrier discovery; Rule 45 to the shipper (the shortest path to the broker); network program-office subpoena where archetype B.
- **Days 30–60:** candidate-broker subpoenas (vetting file first); factor/TMS/ELD/monitoring-vendor subpoenas; deficiency letters wired to motion-to-compel drafts on the Boehne-fight documents (load history, alert logs, override records) — hand off to discovery-manager / motion-practice-manager for filing-ready formatting.
- **Days 60–90:** amendment decision (broker / network defendant / § 14916 count per § 8), 30(b)(6) notices with topic lists, deposition order with the witness each blind-eye finding belongs to.
- **Contingency branches:** what changes if the shipper subpoena comes back empty, if the broker denies the relationship (named-but-denying module), if the network claims the contractor defense.

Jurisdiction-specific, session-verified (module 7 output only — no remembered law):

Caribe Transport II*, No. 24-1238 (U.S. May 14, 2026) — which held negligent-selection claims against brokers are saved by the FAAAA's safety exception, making the theory viable nationwide — stated as the current post-Montgomery rule in this circuit, citations verified this session.
- § 14916 count readiness: the Landstar-line defense as it stands, the counters this record supports, and the PLEAD NOW / HOLD FOR AMENDMENT recommendation (imported from the double-broker-screen Phase 4 analysis where that module ran).
- Network-liability theories for archetype B: agency/control, joint employment, negligent selection/undertaking, direct network-design negligence — each with the controlling or best-available authority and the control evidence from this record that feeds it.
- The defense playbook anticipated (FAAAA motion, contractor-independence motion, empty-chair the driver) and the record-based counter to each.
- Confidence tags carried through from the legal-research chain (VERIFIED-HIGH / VERIFIED / UNVERIFIED) with the attorney sign-off reminder.

## § 9 — What This Record Sets Up

- Case-value drivers this work-up changed (parties added, coverage reached, punitive exposure, story quality).
- Where module 11 ran: **collectability** — what assets exist, where they went, and whether a successor entity or a fraudulent-transfer theory reaches them — plus the retained-expert recommendation for the equipment graph, stated with the specific questions the expert should be asked and what the answer is worth.

## Synthesis rules

1. **Answer first.** Every section opens with its conclusion in plain language; support follows.
2. **Cite the module, don't repeat it.** One devastating example in the body; the itemized record stays in the module artifact.
3. **Cross-reference by number.** Findings (§ 3) ↔ Ledger rows (§ 6) ↔ Campaign asks (§ 7) form a closed loop — every finding has a collection path, every ask has a purpose, nothing dangles.
4. **Clean findings stay in.** NOT INDICATED screens are stated. Credibility is the product.
5. **No new facts at synthesis.** If a sentence needs a fact no module produced, go back and produce it in the module — retrieve → save → write.
